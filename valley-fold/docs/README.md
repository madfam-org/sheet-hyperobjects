# Valley fold

| | Title | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| en | Valley fold | A square folded once toward its front. The crease sinks like a valley, and the fold angle is positive. |
| es | Pliegue en valle | Un cuadrado doblado una vez hacia su frente. El pliegue se hunde como un valle y su ángulo es positivo. |
| fr | Pli vallée | Un carré plié une fois vers son recto. Le pli se creuse comme une vallée et son angle est positif. |
| pt | Dobra em vale | Um quadrado dobrado uma vez em direção à frente. O vinco afunda como um vale e seu ângulo é positivo. |

The valley fold is the first fold of every origami diagram. Lay the square in front of you and
fold part of it toward you along a line, so the two parts close like a book. Open it slightly and
look: the crease sinks below the paper around it, like the floor of a valley.

## Geometry and sign convention

The sheet is a square of side `size` (150 mm by default) in the sheet-local frame, lower-left
corner at the origin, y up. The crease runs either along the **diagonal**, from `(0, 0)` to
`(size, size)`, or as a **book fold** down the middle, from `(size/2, 0)` to `(size/2, size)`. The
arrangement splits the square into two faces along it.

The crease carries assignment `V` and a **positive** fold angle, the FOLD 1.2 convention used by
the sheet document (spec v1 §1): the fold angle is the deviation from flat in degrees, positive
for a valley (the faces rotate toward the **front** side) and negative for a mountain. A square
folded flat carries `+180`. The `angle` parameter (30–180°) sets that value, so you can stop the
fold half-way.

**Front and back.** A sheet lying flat has its front face looking up (+Y in the world frame). The
default stock, kami, is coloured on the front only (red) and white on the back, as the stock
card's appearance block records. In a valley fold the two red front faces close onto each other
and the white back ends up outside. That is how you recognise a valley fold in the hand, and it is
exactly what the `mountain-fold` cartridge reverses.

## What the engine simulates

The fold program has one step, *Valley-fold along the diagonal* (or *in half, edge to edge*), in
`physical` mode. During the step the engine drives the hinge toward its target with the stock's
crease stiffness raised by the hand factor, as a folding hand does. The paper between the hinges
is a physical sheet with the stock's bending stiffness and weight. Once the hinge is held past the
stock's yield angle (`crease_yield_deg`, 35° for kami), plasticity starts to move its rest angle:
the crease takes a set. This cartridge holds the fold at the end. To see what the crease remembers
after it is let go, open `crease-and-unfold`.

The document also carries a `target:folded` frame: the exact rigid pose of the folded state,
computed by the kernel's face tree (`pliego.kinematics`). The keystone and the engine benchmarks
can compare a simulated state against it. Every edge keeps its length in that frame, because
folding never stretches paper.

## Making one

1. Cut a square (export `svg`, `dxf`, `pdf` or `hpgl`). The valley line is drawn in the valley
   style of the exporter's palette. For origami paper you only need the outline.
2. Coloured side up, bring one corner onto the opposite corner (diagonal) or one edge onto the
   opposite edge (book fold).
3. Hold the edges together and press the crease flat from the middle outward.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Meaning | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `size` | side of the square (mm) | 150 |
| `stock` | `kami`, `foil-paper`, `copy-80`, `tant` | `kami` |
| `fold_line` | `diagonal` (corner to corner) or `book` (edge to edge) | `diagonal` |
| `angle` | target fold angle (°); 180 is folded flat | 180 |

Presets: `book-fold`, `half-open` (90°).

## History and sources

The valley fold is a traditional technique, not anyone's design. The convention for writing it
down in diagrams (a dashed line for a valley fold, a line of dashes and dots for a mountain fold,
arrows for the motion) is the Yoshizawa–Randlett system. Akira Yoshizawa introduced it in his 1954
book *Atarashii Origami Geijutsu*; Samuel Randlett and Robert Harbin later adopted and extended it
in their English-language books (Wikipedia, «Yoshizawa–Randlett system»,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshizawa%E2%80%93Randlett_system). This cartridge reproduces no
diagram; its geometry is generated by its own `main.py`.
