# Perforated tear-off stub

| | Title | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| en | Perforated tear-off stub | A card with a perforated stub, like a ticket: pull the stub and the strain gathers at the bridges until they tear. |
| es | Talón desprendible perforado | Una tarjeta con un talón perforado, como un boleto: al jalar el talón, la deformación se concentra en los puentes hasta que se rompen. |
| fr | Talon détachable perforé | Une carte avec un talon perforé, comme un billet : on tire sur le talon et la déformation se concentre sur les ponts jusqu'à ce qu'ils se déchirent. |
| pt | Canhoto destacável picotado | Um cartão com um canhoto picotado, como um ingresso: ao puxar o canhoto, a deformação se concentra nas pontes até que se rompam. |

A perforation is a line cut most of the way through: a row of short cuts separated by small
uncut **bridges**. The bridges hold the stub on while the card is handled, and they are weak enough
to give way along the line, and nowhere else, when you pull. Tickets, coupons, cheque books, stamps
and tear-out pages all use one.

## Geometry

The sheet is a card `width × height` (150 × 60 mm by default). The perforation is a straight line
across it at `u = width − stub`, which leaves a stub `stub` mm wide (40 mm by default) on the right.

The line is written with `Sheet.perforate(..., kind="cut")`: treatment `perforation`, assignment
`C`. The assignment says the line is designed to separate; the treatment says how it is made and
how it behaves (sheet-document spec v1 §4). The arrangement treats it as a line that splits the
card into two faces, the body and the stub. The cut-and-bridge pitch is not drawn in the
geometry: it comes from the stock card (`fabrication.perforation`, 3 mm cuts and 1 mm bridges for
`cardstock-160`), and the exporters use it to draw the perforation as a dashed cut.

## The tear: joins, driver and control

There is no fold program. The object is driven by one **distance control**, `tear` (0 to `pull`
mm, labelled *Pull the stub*), wired as follows:

- `pin` **body-hold**: every engine vertex along the body's left edge is fixed where it starts;
- `pin` **stub-grip**: every vertex along the stub's right edge is fixed to the `translate`
  mechanism **pull-stub**;
- `translate` **pull-stub** moves its pins along world +X (the plane of the card, away from the
  body) by `control / max × travel`. Here `max` and `travel` are both `pull` (10 mm by default),
  so the control reads directly in millimetres.

Per the sheet-document spec (§4, §5.2), the engine treats a perforation as a hinge whose stiffness
is multiplied by the stock's `perf_factor` and which separates under strain once it passes the
stock's `tear_strain`. The kernel derives `perf_factor` as the bridge fraction of the perforation
pitch, `bridge / (cut + bridge)`: 1 / (3 + 1) = 0.25 for this card. Only a quarter of the line is
material. Pulling the stub puts the whole card in tension, but the load across the line is carried
by those bridges, so the strain concentrates in them and reaches `tear_strain` (1.5 % for
`cardstock-160`, an estimate on the stock card) there first. The line tears, and the rest of the
card does not.

## Making one

1. Export the card (`svg`, `dxf`, `pdf` or `hpgl`). The perforation is on its own layer; the
   exporters draw it as a dashed cut using the stock card's cut and bridge lengths.
2. Cut it with a perforating wheel along a ruler, a cutting plotter's perforation setting, or by
   hand: 3 mm cuts with a craft knife, leaving 1 mm bridges.
3. Fold the stub back and forth along the perforation once or twice, then pull it off. The
   folding starts the bridges breaking and gives a cleaner tear.

Finer pitches (more, shorter bridges) tend to tear more cleanly, and heavy card tears more easily
with longer cuts or shorter bridges.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Meaning | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `width` | card width (mm) | 150 |
| `height` | card height, the length of the perforation (mm) | 60 |
| `stub` | stub width, perforation to edge (mm); at most half the card | 40 |
| `pull` | how far the control drags the stub (mm) | 10 |
| `stock` | `cardstock-160`, `copy-80`, `cardstock-250`, `kraft-120` | `cardstock-160` |

Presets: `ticket-copy` (a 100 × 50 mm coupon in copy paper with a 25 mm stub), `wide-stub` (70 mm).

## History and sources

Perforation as a way of separating printed items is usually traced to postage stamps: Henry Archer
devised a perforating machine in Britain in the late 1840s, and perforated stamps were issued there
from 1854 (Wikipedia, «Postage stamp separation»,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postage_stamp_separation). This cartridge models the technique, not
any historical stamp or ticket; its geometry is generated by its own `main.py`. The physics figures
are from the commons' stock cards and the sheet-document spec v1 (§4, §5.2).
