# Page turn

| | Title | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| en | Page turn | One leaf hinged at its spine. Turn it, and the paper bends as it travels from right to left. |
| es | Pasar la página | Una hoja sujeta por el lomo. Al pasarla, el papel se curva mientras viaja de derecha a izquierda. |
| fr | Tourner la page | Un feuillet tenu par le dos. Quand on le tourne, le papier se courbe en passant de droite à gauche. |
| pt | Virar a página | Uma folha presa pela lombada. Ao virá-la, o papel se curva enquanto viaja da direita para a esquerda. |

The page turn is the first rung of the commons and its simplest object: one rectangular leaf, one
edge held at a spine, one control. It has no crease and no cut, yet it is already a physical
object. The way a page curls, lags behind your finger and then drops flat on the other side
depends on the paper it is made of, and that is what this cartridge lets you see.

## Geometry

The leaf is a rectangle `width × height` (A5 portrait by default, 148 × 210 mm), drawn in the
sheet-local frame with its lower-left corner at the origin and y up. Its left edge, from
`(0, 0)` to `(0, height)`, is the **spine**. The right edge, at `u = width`, is the **fore-edge**.
The crease pattern is a single face bounded by four outline edges (`B`), so there is nothing to
fold and nothing to cut except the outline.

The grain runs up the page (`grain_deg = 90`, long grain), as it does in a well-made book. Pages
then bend easily around an axis parallel to the spine, which is the direction a page turns.

## Mechanics: what the engine simulates

The cartridge builds its leaf with the kernel's book generator, `pliego.book.leaf`, which writes
three things into the sheet document:

- a **`pin` join along the spine**. Every vertex of the engine mesh within the stock's collision
  thickness of the spine segment is fixed where it starts, so the spine cannot move;
- a **`leaf` mechanism** naming the sheet and its spine. It moves a drive point on the leaf's free
  edge from 0° (the page lies flat to the right) to 180° (turned to the left);
- an **angle control**, `turn` (0–180°, default 0), labelled *Turn the page* in the four languages.

Nothing else is rigid. The page is a physical sheet: the engine integrates its bending stiffness
(`D_md` along the grain, `D_cd` across it) and its weight (areal density), all taken from the
stock's derived physics in the document (`pliego:stocks`). So the same turn should look different
on different papers. The table gives what that physics predicts; it is not a measured result:

| Stock | What the physics predicts |
|---|---|
| Copy paper 80 g/m² | a soft curl that follows the drive point and settles flat |
| Kozo washi 30 g/m² | a light, floppy page that drapes and lags well behind the drive |
| Kami (red and white) | the same curl, with the red recto and white verso showing as the page flips |
| Cardstock 160 g/m² | a stiff leaf that turns almost as a flat board |

The face colours come from each stock card's `appearance` block. The recto is the front face (it
looks up, +Y, when the page lies at 0°). The verso is the back face, so turning the page shows it.

Honest limits: the leaf is one sheet hinged at one edge. There is no stack of pages under it, no
binding thread and no board. Those belong to `codex-signatures`, `saddle-stitch` and the other
book structures higher up the catalog.

## Making one

1. Cut a rectangle of the chosen paper to `width × height` (export `svg`, `dxf`, `pdf` or `hpgl`;
   the only line is the cut outline).
2. Hold the left edge flat on the table under a ruler, or tape it to a card spine.
3. Lift the fore-edge with one finger and turn it over. Compare a sheet of copy paper with a
   sheet of 160 g/m² card cut to the same size.

Keep the grain parallel to the spine. A long-grain sheet turns more easily than a short-grain
one, for the reason given above.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Meaning | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `width` | page width, spine to fore-edge (mm) | 148 |
| `height` | page height, the length of the spine (mm) | 210 |
| `stock` | paper: `copy-80`, `washi-kozo-30`, `kami`, `cardstock-160` | `copy-80` |

Presets: `a6` (105 × 148 mm), `a4-card` (210 × 297 mm in 160 g/m² card), `washi` (kozo washi).

## Interfaces

`spine` (the hinge edge, neutral) and `page_edge` (the fore-edge, neutral). Book structures compose
leaves through these two edges. A leaf mounts on a spine, and the fore-edge is the edge a reader
(or a pull tab) drives.

## History and sources

The leaf hinged at a spine is the unit of the **codex**, the book form that gradually displaced the
scroll in the Roman world during the first centuries CE (Wikipedia, «Codex»,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex). This cartridge models that unit and nothing more. It does
not reproduce any historical book.
