## What it is

A schoolyard noisemaker folded from one sheet. Held by its two points and snapped down hard, the
folded pocket inside flies out and the air it pushes makes the bang.

## Geometry

The sheet lies landscape, `W × H` (A4 by default); `m = H/2`, `c = W/2`.

1. Fold in half the long way and unfold: the centre line `y = m`.
2. Fold the four corners to the centre line along 45° lines, each through the midpoint `m` of the
   adjacent edge.
3. Fold in half along the centre line, corners inside: a strip `m` high.
4. Fold in half the short way and unfold: the line `x = c`.
5. Fold both ends down along 45° lines from the top centre `(c, m)`: the strip becomes a
   diamond-like shape with both points down.
6. Fold in half backwards along `x = c`.

## The fold program

The program is `kinematic`, 8 steps, all simple folds; every state is an exact rigid state (closure
below 10⁻¹² mm). The studio shows the folded popper standing with its points down.

**The bang needs physics.** The snap is a flap that turns inside out in a few milliseconds and the
air it drives. Both belong to the physics engine and its air model; the program stops in the folded
state, ready to snap.

## Making one by hand

1. Fold a landscape A4 sheet in half the long way and unfold.
2. Fold all four corners to the centre line.
3. Fold in half along the centre line, corners inside.
4. Fold in half the short way and unfold.
5. Fold both ends down along lines from the top centre, so they point down.
6. Fold in half backwards.
7. Hold the two points together and snap your arm down.

## History and sources

The Public Paperfolding History Project describes two closely related forms, the single-barrelled
and the double-barrelled paper banger, and finds the banger in *Jinjo Kouto Shogaku Shuko
Seisakuzu* (Handicrafts for ordinary higher elementary schools) by Hideyoshi Okayama (Japan, 1903).

- The Public Paperfolding History Project, "The paper banger":
