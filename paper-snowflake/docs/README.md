## Folding to a 30° wedge

Every fold passes through the centre of the square:

1. **Diagonal** (valley): the square becomes a right triangle — two layers, a straight angle of 180°
   at the centre.
2. **One third over the middle** (valley) and 3. **the other third behind** (mountain): the 180° is
   split into three 60° sectors stacked on each other — a 60° wedge of six layers. Folding the
   thirds one in front and one behind keeps the stack balanced.
4. **In half** (valley): a 30° wedge of twelve layers.

Cutting the 60° wedge alone would give three mirror lines and three-fold rotation; the last fold adds
the mirror lines between them, so the unfolded paper has **six-fold rotation with six mirrors**
(the dihedral group D6) — the symmetry of a snow crystal's hexagonal lattice.

## The cut

The cartridge cuts four regions through all twelve layers at once (`pliego.foldcut`, which maps
each region back through every layer and keeps exactly the paper whose folded image lies outside
them):

- the **outline** beyond each arm: the arm's straight sides, its pointed tip and, near the tip,
  a small spike that leans at 60°, parallel to the neighbouring arm;
- the **pores** between the hub, the arm and a branch: each branch leaves its arm at 60° and runs
  into the mirror fold, where it meets the twin branch of the next arm — so the six arms are held
  together by a hexagonal ring as well as by the hub;
- a **diamond** on each arm's axis inside the hub, and a round **centre hole**.

The motif is an original, generic design generated by `main.py`; it is not copied from any
published template.

## Checked before it is drawn

The cartridge runs `pliego.papercut.analyze` on the unfolded sheet and refuses to build unless the
paper is **one connected piece** (no island falls out) and **every material bridge is at least the
stock's minimum feature** (1.0 mm for copy paper, kami and washi; 1.5 mm for tissue). The document
declares that minimum as `pliego:meta.min_feature_mm`. The manifest also carries an error-level constraint that
stops rendering when the hub would be too small for the chosen arm width.

## What the engine shows

The fold program re-folds the cut sheet along its creases in the original order (four steps) and
then releases every crease: the snowflake opens out. Version 1 documents describe the sheet after
cutting, so the program shows the folding on the cut paper; in the real craft the cut is made while
the paper is folded.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `size` | side of the starting square, mm |
| `arm_width` | full width of each arm, mm |
| `hub` | hub radius as a fraction of the arm length |
| `branch` | where the branches leave each arm, as a fraction of its length |
| `spikes` | add the spike pair near each tip |
| `stock` | copy paper, kami, kozo washi or tissue paper |

## Making it

Print the folding diagram or simply fold a square of paper as above, then trace one 30° sector of the
exported cut lines onto the top layer and cut with sharp scissors or a craft knife on a mat.
Twelve layers of copy paper are thick: a knife cuts the inner pores more cleanly than scissors, and
tissue paper cuts most easily of all. A plotter or laser can instead cut the flat, unfolded pattern
from the SVG or DXF export.
