## What it is

The slotted case is the ordinary corrugated shipping box: one blank of board, four body panels
in a row closed by a glued manufacturer's joint, and flaps on the top and bottom edges separated
by slots. This hyperobject carries the family's common variants:

- **Regular slotted** (FEFCO 0201 style): every flap the same length, half the box's width, so
  the two outer flaps meet in the middle.
- **Half-slotted** (FEFCO 0200 style): bottom flaps only; an open-top case or a tray.
- **Full overlap** (FEFCO 0203 style): flaps as long as the box is wide, so the outer flaps
  cover the whole top and overlap completely — stiffer and better stacked.
- **Telescope** (mode `telescope`): two half-slotted trays, a base and a shallower lid that
  slides over it.

Boards: single wall E, B or C flute, or BC double wall (Pliego stock cards derived from the
corrugation model; their physics is described on the cards and in the physics memo §(h)).

## Geometry

Everything is generated by `pliego.dieline` from the **inside** dimensions L × W × D and the
board's caliper t (read from the stock card):

- A board folded 90° turns about its centre line, so each body panel is the inside dimension
  plus t: front and back L + t, ends W + t, body height D + t.
- Regular and half-slotted flaps are (W + t)/2 long; full-overlap flaps W + t.
- Slots are 2t wide (at least 2 mm) and run from the flap edge to the fold, centred on the body
  folds; the front panel's flaps are trimmed by half a slot at the free edge too.
- The manufacturer's joint (`glue` mm, at most half the depth) is a chamfered flap on the last
  end panel, glued inside the front panel's free edge. Its glue zone is part of the dieline.
- The outer flaps are drawn tilted by asin(t / flap length) in the closed state: a
  zero-thickness preview cannot stack them, so they rest on the inner flaps by that angle.
- The telescope lid's inside is the base's outside (inside + 2t), so it slides over the base.

The cut outline is the boundary of the union of the panels (slots and notches come out of the
gaps), every fold is a crease-rule score with a stable name (`front-end-1-fold`,
`end-2-top-flap-fold`…), every free cut edge is named after its panel, and the document carries
a `pliego:dielines` block (panels, bleed and safe areas, glue zones, inside-dimension lines and
the flute arrow) that the dieline export profile draws on named, spot-coloured layers.

These are centre-line design rules, not a converting plant's specification: plants add their
own allowances (crease-rule offsets, joint widths, slot widths).

## The fold program

A kinematic program (the studio preview plays it in full):

1. fold the body into a tube and glue the joint;
2. fold in the bottom end flaps, then close the bottom with the outer flaps;
3. the same for the top (not in the half-slotted style).

No fold meets another inside the board (flaps are separated by slots), so the blank is a face
tree: rigidly foldable, nothing for the motion solver to close. The sheet lies print side down
and the case erects off the table, lying on its front panel. **What needs the physics engine:**
the glue weld of the joint (here a glue zone, not a join), the outer flaps really resting on the
inner ones (board thickness and contact), crush and stacking strength.

## Making one by hand

Cut the blank from single-wall board with a knife against a steel rule; score every fold with a
blunt creasing tool on the inside (unprinted) face, along the flutes where you can. Glue the
joint with PVA or hot melt, square the tube, fold the inner flaps then the outer flaps and tape
the seam. For the telescope, fold the base and the lid, then slide the lid down over the base.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `style` | regular · half · full | flap style (one-piece mode) |
| `length`, `width`, `depth` | 100–600, 80–500, 50–600 mm | inside dimensions (width ≤ length) |
| `lid_depth` | 30–600 mm | telescope lid depth (at most the base depth) |
| `board` | E · B · C · BC | corrugated board stock |
| `glue` | 20–50 mm | manufacturer's joint width |

Presets: small parcel (E flute), moving box (C flute), heavy duty (BC double wall).

## History and sources

Corrugated board was patented for packaging in the 1870s and the slotted case became the
standard shipper in the twentieth century; FEFCO (the European federation of corrugated board
manufacturers) and ESBO publish the international code of box styles in which 0200, 0201 and
0203 name these styles. This cartridge cites the codes as identifiers only and reproduces no
catalogue drawing.

- FEFCO, *International Fibreboard Case Code* (code list) — https://www.fefco.org/
- Wikipedia, *Corrugated fiberboard* (flute designations after Foster 1997) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrugated_fiberboard
- Rogalka, Grabski & Garbowski 2023, *Sensors* 23:6242 (measured flute heights) — https://doi.org/10.3390/s23136242
- Pliego physics memo §(h), *Board, corrugation and foam* (in the Pliego repository).
