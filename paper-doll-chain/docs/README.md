## What it is

Pleat a strip of paper into an accordion, draw one figure on the top panel so that its hands reach
both folds, and cut through every layer. Open the strip and the figures come out in a row, holding
hands. The parts that touch the folds are never cut, so they hold the chain together. This
cartridge carries three original figures: a **person**, a **figure in a dress** (joined at the hands
and at the hem) and a **heart** (joined at the lobes).

## Geometry

The strip is `panels × panel_width` long and `height` high. It is pleated from its right end:
each fold takes everything to its right over or under (valley and mountain in turn), so the
accordion ends on the first panel with one layer per panel (`pliego.simplefold`; the script checks
the layer count).

A figure is a half outline in the panel's unit box: across from the centre line (0) to the fold
(0.5), up from 0 to 1. It runs from the top of the centre line to the bottom of it, and the
segments where it lies on the fold are the joints. Mirrored about the panel's centre line, it gives
the whole figure. The removed regions are the gaps between the figure and the two folds: above the
first joint, below the last, and, for the dress, between the hands and the hem on each side. They
cross the folds by 1 mm, so no region edge lies on a fold line. The script refuses a figure that
never reaches the fold, and a joint shorter than twice the minimum feature.

`pliego.foldcut` maps the cut through every layer and opens the strip, and `pliego.papercut.analyze`
must find one connected chain with every bridge at least the stock's minimum feature (1.0 mm for
copy paper, kraft and kami; 1.5 mm for cardstock).

## The fold program

Two steps: pleat the strip into an accordion one panel wide; cut one figure through every layer,
then open the chain and press it flat. The program ends with the chain held flat by the hand. It
does not let go: following spec §5.6, a release would leave the pleats standing at about the paper's
yield angle. Version 1 documents describe the sheet after cutting, so the preview shows the
pleating on the cut paper.

## Making one by hand

1. Cut a strip of paper and pleat it back and forth into panels of equal width.
2. Trace the figure from the export onto the top panel, with the hands (and the hem, for the dress)
   running right up to both folds.
3. Cut through all the layers, never along the folds at the joints.
4. Open the chain and press it flat.

Copy paper cuts well up to about eight layers with sharp scissors. A plotter, craft cutter or laser
can cut the flat chain from the SVG, DXF or HPGL export instead.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `figure` | person, figure in a dress, or heart |
| `panels` | number of figures, one per panel and per layer |
| `panel_width` | width of each panel, mm |
| `height` | strip height, mm |
| `stock` | copy paper, kraft, kami or cardstock |

## History and sources

Chains of figures cut from a pleated strip are a familiar paper craft. This cartridge describes the
technique generically and makes no historical claim. The mathematics of cutting folded paper is
studied in the paper below.

- E. D. Demaine, M. L. Demaine and A. Lubiw, "Folding and cutting paper", in *Discrete and
  Computational Geometry* (JCDCG '98), Lecture Notes in Computer Science 1763, Springer (2000).
