## What it is

A lucky star is folded from one long, narrow strip. Tie a loose knot near one end and flatten it:
the knot is a regular pentagon. Wrap the long end round and round the pentagon, folding over each
side in turn, and tuck the last bit under a layer. Then push each side in at its middle with a
fingernail and the flat pentagon puffs into a small, round five-pointed star. Many people fold them
by the jarful.

## Geometry

A knot in a strip of width w flattens into a regular pentagon of side s = w / sin 72°: a regular
pentagon "may be created from just a strip of paper by tying an overhand knot" and flattening it
(Wikipedia, «Pentagon»). Each pass of the strip covers the pentagon from one side to the diagonal
parallel to that side, an isosceles trapezoid with bases s and φ·s (φ the golden ratio) and height
w. Folding over the side that ends the pass lays the strip across the next trapezoid, turned round
the pentagon. Unfolded, the fold lines zig-zag along the strip at 72° and 108° to its edges, so the
strip is a row of trapezoids, alternately short side down and short side up. There are
5 × `wraps` + 1 of them; the strip's ends are cut along the same slant.

The script checks this by folding: every trapezoid, reflected across the fold lines before it, lands
with its four corners on the pentagon's corners.

## The fold program

1. **Knot** (folds 1–3): the first three folds make the pentagon.
2. **Wrap** (one fold per step): every fold is a mountain fold of 180°, the print staying outside.
3. **Tuck**: the last fold, and the end slips under a layer.
4. **Puff**: pinch each side in at its middle.

The program is kinematic: the strip is a chain of rigid trapezoids, so every in-between state is
exact.

**Preview and engine.** The knot, the wraps and the tuck play in the studio preview, which folds the
layers in order; the knot's threading of the strip through its own loop is not modelled. The last
step, puffing, bends the paper into the star's curved faces and needs the physics engine; the
preview stops at the flat pentagon.

## Making one by hand

1. Export the strip (`svg`, `pdf`, `dxf` or `hpgl`) or just cut a strip about 1 cm by 27 cm.
2. Tie a loose overhand knot near one end and gently pull it flat into a pentagon; fold the short
   tail behind.
3. Wrap the long end round the pentagon, folding along its edges, keeping it snug.
4. Tuck the end under the last layer. Hold the star by two points and press each side in with a
   fingernail, one after another, until it puffs.

Presets: `mini` (7 mm), `big-full` (20 mm, 5 turns).

## History and sources

The lucky star is a folk paper craft folded in many countries, often from wrapping strips or
purpose-cut paper. We found no reliable source for its origin, so it is credited here to that folk
tradition in general. The pentagon from a knotted strip is a classical construction: Wikipedia,
«Pentagon», https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentagon. The fold lines are computed by this cartridge.
