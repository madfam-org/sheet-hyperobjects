## What it is

A modular model: two sheets, each a half square, folded into mirror-image units and locked together
by folding each unit's halves across the other. It is the first two-sheet object in this lane: the
document holds two sheets, `unit-a` and `unit-b`.

## Geometry

Each unit is a `4u × 2u` rectangle, `u = size / 4`.

1. Fold in half crosswise and unfold (the line `x = 2u`).
2. Fold in half lengthwise: a strip `4u × u`.
3. Fold opposite corners at 45°: the bottom-left corner up, the top-right corner down. The strip
   becomes a parallelogram with 45° ends.
4. Fold the halves at 45°, along the lines `y = x − u` and `y = x − 2u`: the left half turns down,
   the right half up. Each half becomes a right isosceles triangle (a blade), and the unit is a Z.

Unit B follows the mirror-image instructions. It is then turned over, given a quarter turn and laid
under unit A with both centres at `(2u, u/2)`, one paper thickness (0.4 mm) below. The two Z units
crossed at 90° make the pinwheel: a square centre and four blades.

## The fold program

The program is `kinematic`, 10 steps over two sheets. Steps 1–3 for unit A, then for unit B, then
the assembly: unit B's halves are folded across unit A, then unit A's halves across unit B. Every
state is an exact rigid state (closure below 10⁻¹² mm on each sheet).

Unit B is placed crossed under unit A from the start. The studio's preview poses each sheet about
its own first face, so sheets do not travel; a placement step would need a mechanism, and none fits
a sheet that is also being folded. In a model without paper thickness, tucking a flap *under*
another and laying it on top occupy the same place: the program shows where each flap goes, not
which layer it slides between.

## Making one by hand

1. Cut a square in half. Fold each half in half crosswise and unfold, then in half lengthwise.
2. On unit A, fold the bottom-left corner up and the top-right corner down. On unit B, do the
   mirror image.
3. Lay unit B, turned over and turned a quarter, under unit A.
4. Fold unit B's two halves across unit A and tuck them under unit A's corner flaps. Turn over and
   do the same with unit A's halves.

## History and sources

The two-unit shuriken is a widely taught traditional model; the Origami Resource Center presents it
as a traditional design, with the same unit and assembly sequence used here. No earlier written
record was found for this lane, so no date is claimed.
