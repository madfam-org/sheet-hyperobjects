## What it is

A palm-sized boomerang for indoors. Lay it on your flat hand, or hold it upright between finger and
thumb, and flick one blade tip hard with the other hand: it spins away, its path curves, and it
comes back. Three blades give more lift than two. The tips are bent up a little by hand before
throwing.

## Geometry

Three blades 120° apart. Each is a strip `blade_width` wide whose edges run parallel to its axis
out to a semicircular tip, the outermost point `blade_length` from the centre. Between neighbouring
blades the edges meet in a fillet arc of radius `fillet`, tangent to both (its centre lies on the
bisector at (`blade_width` / 2 + `fillet`) / sin 60° from the centre). A small kiss-cut ring marks
the centre. The printed face carries the colour, a light band along each blade and a curved mark
showing the spin, counter-clockwise from the front.

## The mechanism

The boomerang is a kinematic sheet turned by a `rotate` mechanism about its centre on the `spin`
control.

**Preview and engine.** The shape and the spin play in the studio preview. The flight needs the
engine's air model (wave 3): each spinning blade makes more lift at the top of its turn than at the
bottom, and gyroscopic precession turns that into the curve that brings it back. The slight bend
of the tips is done by hand and is a bend, not a crease, so the cut file has no fold lines.

## Making one by hand

1. Export the outline (`svg` carries the colour; `pdf`, `dxf` and `hpgl` the cut line) on stiff card.
2. Cut it out, keeping the inner corners round.
3. Bend each blade tip up a little, about a fingertip's length, all the same way.
4. Hold it flat on your palm or upright between finger and thumb and flick one tip sharply. If it
   dives, bend the tips a little more; if it climbs, a little less.

## History and sources

Boomerang-like throwing sticks have been used for hunting by some Aboriginal Australian groups, and
the returning boomerang was known in parts of Australia but not everywhere. Modern sport boomerangs
often have three or four wings, which give more lift than two, and the return comes from the
gyroscopic precession of the spinning blades (Wikipedia, «Boomerang»,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boomerang). This small card toy only borrows the returning principle:
it is not a traditional boomerang, and its outline is computed by this cartridge.
