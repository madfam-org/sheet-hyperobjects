## Geometry

The sheet is an `n × n` lattice of squares of side `L`. Every interior lattice vertex `(i, j)`
carries one straight through cut of length `2L − 2w`, centred on the vertex: **horizontal** where
`i + j` is even, **vertical** where it is odd. Vertices on the outline carry the half cut that
reaches the edge of the sheet. The result: along every edge shared by two squares the paper is cut
except for one ligament of width `w` at one end. Each square therefore keeps one hinge at each of its
four corners, and each hinge joins it to a different neighbour.

The pattern is generated by this cartridge's `main.py`; no published cut file is copied.

## Mechanics

With ideal point hinges this is the *rotating-squares* mechanism of Grima and Evans: neighbouring
squares rotate by `+φ` and `−φ`, opening diamond-shaped pores between them. The distance between
the centres of two neighbouring squares grows from `L` to `L·(cos φ + sin φ)` in **both** lattice
directions at once, so the in-plane Poisson ratio is `ν = −1` throughout the opening, and the open
area grows by the factor `(cos φ + sin φ)²` (largest at `φ = 45°`, where the pores are squares).

Real ligaments have a width, so they bend and twist rather than pivoting about a point: a thin sheet
can also buckle out of plane under tension, which is the subject of later kirigami research.

## What the engine shows

The document is a **physical** model. Version 1 of the sheet document cannot place the two sides
of a cut apart in its initial frame, so the sheet starts flat and closed on the table; the engine
splits the cut vertices when it builds its mesh, and the opening happens in the simulation.

- The centre of the left-middle square is fixed with a `pin` join (`anchor`).
- The centre of the right-middle square is pinned to a `translate` mechanism (`pull-apart`),
  driven along +x by the **Pull** control. Its travel is the ideal-hinge stretch
  `(n − 1)·L·(cos φ + sin φ − 1)` for the chosen opening angle.

Single points are pinned, not whole edges, on purpose: clamping a full edge holds one square's
rotation at zero, and in the ideal mechanism every square turns by the same amount, so a clamped
edge would lock the lattice. Watch the sheet widen as it is pulled.

The cartridge runs Pliego's papercut check on the cut sheet and refuses to build if the material
falls apart into islands or a ligament is narrower than the stock's minimum feature
(`pliego:meta.min_feature_mm` declares that minimum in the document).

## Parameters

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `units` | squares per side |
| `unit` | square size `L`, mm |
| `ligament` | ligament width `w`, mm (at least the stock's minimum feature, at most `L/4`) |
| `open_angle` | square rotation `φ` at full pull, degrees |
| `stock` | cardstock 160 g/m², copy paper, kraft or polypropylene |

## Making it

Cut the SVG or DXF export on a plotter or laser cutter, or with a scalpel and a steel rule. Every
cut is a straight slit; stop each one exactly at its end point — a cut that runs over severs the
ligament. Polypropylene sheet survives many openings; paper ligaments fatigue after a few dozen.
Lay the sheet flat, hold one square and pull the opposite one: the sheet grows in width too.
