## What it is

The paper kabuto: a helmet with two horns, folded from one square. Folded from a newspaper square
(about 500–600 mm), it fits a child's head.

## Geometry

The square is turned 45° (a diamond); `d` is half its diagonal and the diamond's corners sit at
`(0, ±d)`, `(±d, 0)`.

1. The top corner folds down to the bottom one along the horizontal diagonal: a triangle, point
   down.
2. The left and right corners fold down to the bottom point. Their fold lines leave the top
   centre at ±45°, so the triangle becomes a square (a diamond) of side `d`.
3. The two front flaps fold up along the horizontal line at height `−d/2`, so their points meet
   the top.
4. **Horns.** Each point folds outwards along a line through the flap's lower inner corner
   `(0, −d/2)`. The line makes `horn` degrees with the horizontal; at the default 67.5° the point
   comes out at 45°, up and out.
5. The front bottom corner folds up to height `−d/2`, then up again along the line at `−0.62 d`:
   the brim. The back corner folds up inside the helmet.

## The fold program

The program is `kinematic`, 7 steps, all simple folds; every state is an exact rigid state (closure
below 10⁻¹² mm). The studio shows the flat helmet standing upright. **Wearing it** means opening the
bottom into a rounded crown, which curves the paper and needs the physics engine.

## Making one by hand

1. Fold a square in half along a diagonal, point down.
2. Fold the left and right corners down to the bottom point.
3. Fold the two front flaps up so their points meet the top.
4. Fold those points outwards and up: the horns.
5. Fold the front bottom corner up to just under the horns, then fold it up again.
6. Fold the back corner up inside, and open the helmet from below.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `size` | side of the square, mm (500–600 mm makes a wearable helmet) |
| `horn` | angle of the horn fold line from level, 55–80° |
| `stock` | kami or copy paper 80 g/m² |

## History and sources

The Public Paperfolding History Project records the kabuto (Japanese helmet, samurai helmet) in a
print by Eishin Kikugawa (1787–1867) that shows children folding paper, beside a crane and a boat,
in a form that looks like a more complex kabuto. The kabuto is pictured in *Yochien Ombutsu No Zu*,
the kindergarten material of the Tokyo Women's Normal School (1878). In China, diagrams of the same
design, titled "Pocket", appear in *Zhe zhi tu shuo* (Illustrated Paperfolding, Shanghai, 1914).
