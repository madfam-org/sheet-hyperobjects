## What it is

The simplest fold-and-cut. Fold a sheet in half, draw half of a shape against the fold, cut through
both layers and open it: the shape comes out perfectly symmetric about the fold. This cartridge
carries three original motifs, an **urn**, a **tree** and a **butterfly**, and two ways to cut
them: as a **silhouette** (the shape is kept, everything around it falls away) or as a **window**
(the shape is cut out of a framed card).

## Geometry

The sheet is folded along its vertical centre line (`pliego.simplefold`), which puts both halves
in one stack. Each motif is a half outline in a unit box: it starts on the fold at the top, runs out
and back, and ends on the fold at the bottom. Unfolded, the motif spans at most 88 % of the sheet's width
and 86 % of its height, so the window version keeps a frame around the shape.

- **Silhouette.** The cut region is everything on the folded half outside the half outline, plus
  the motif's detail holes inside it: oval holes in the urn's body with a diamond on the fold, round
  ornaments on the tree (one centred on the fold), and eye-spots on the butterfly's wings.
- **Window.** The cut region is the half outline itself. Inner details would fall out of a window,
  so none are cut.

The cut regions cross the fold by 1 mm, so no region edge lies on the fold line itself.
`pliego.foldcut` maps the cut through both layers and unfolds it, and `pliego.papercut.analyze`
must find one connected piece with every bridge at least the stock's minimum feature; otherwise the
cartridge stops with a message.

## The fold program

Two steps: fold the sheet in half; cut the half motif through both layers, then unfold and press
flat. The program ends with the shape held flat (the hand presses it), as it is mounted or framed.
It does not let go: following spec §5.6, a release would leave the centre crease standing at about
the paper's yield angle. Version 1 documents describe the sheet after cutting, so the preview shows
the fold on the cut paper.

## Making one by hand

1. Fold the paper in half and crease it sharply.
2. Trace the half motif from the export onto the folded sheet, with its straight side on the fold.
3. Cut through both layers with sharp scissors; cut the inner holes with a craft knife on a mat.
4. Open it and press it flat under a book.

A plotter, craft cutter or laser can instead cut the flat, unfolded shape from the SVG, DXF or HPGL
export. Black paper on a light ground shows the silhouette best.

## History and sources

Cutting a folded sheet to obtain a symmetric shape is a widespread, generic papercut technique. This
cartridge describes it generically and makes no claim about any particular tradition. The
mathematics of cutting folded paper (which shapes one straight cut can produce after folding) is
studied in the paper below.

- E. D. Demaine, M. L. Demaine and A. Lubiw, "Folding and cutting paper", in *Discrete and
  Computational Geometry* (JCDCG '98), Lecture Notes in Computer Science 1763, Springer (2000).
