## What it is

A zoetrope is a drum with vertical slits, open at the top, with a strip of pictures inside showing
the stages of one motion. Spin it and look through the slits at the pictures across the drum: each
slit shows one picture for an instant, then the next slit shows the next picture from the same
place, and the pictures move. This cartridge makes the drum, its base and a picture strip with one
of two original cycles: a bouncing ball (squashed when it lands) or a flapping bird.

## Geometry

- **Drum**: a strip of `frames` panels of width 2·r·sin(180°/frames), r = `diameter` / 2, and
  `height` tall. Each panel has a slit in its upper part (0.08 × the panel width, 2.5–5 mm wide),
  above the picture strip. A 12 mm glue tab at the end overlaps the first panel; a small trapezoid
  tab under each panel folds in to be glued onto the base. The panel lines are scored, each turning
  360°/frames, so the drum rolls into an even polygon. The slit band passes the papercut check
  (bridges of at least 3 mm).
- **Picture strip**: the same number of panels, a little narrower so it sits 0.8 mm inside the
  drum, 0.45 × the drum height. Frame k is drawn in panel k: the cycle runs once around the drum.
- **Base disc**: 12 mm wider than the drum, with a kiss-cut cross at the centre for the spindle.

## The mechanism

- **Spinning** (mode `spin`): the assembled zoetrope. The end tab and the bottom tabs are glue
  joins, and the initial frame poses the drum as the polygon (the tabs welded exactly). The three
  sheets are kinematic and turn together on a `rotate` mechanism about the base's centre, on the
  `spin` control.
- **Making it** (mode `make`): the fold program folds the base tabs, rolls the drum and rolls the
  picture strip.

**Preview and engine.** Both modes play in the studio preview. The animation itself is seen by a
person at a few turns a second; the paper's smooth curve between the scores and the friction on the
spindle belong to the physics engine.

## Making one by hand

1. Export the sheets. The `svg` carries the picture strip's drawings; draw your own cycle on a
   blank strip if you like (one stage per panel).
2. Cut the drum, its slits and tabs, the strip and the base. Score the drum's panel lines.
3. Roll the drum, dark side in, and glue the end tab inside the first panel. Fold the bottom tabs
   in and glue them onto the base.
4. Slip the picture strip inside below the slits, pictures facing in. Put a pencil or a pin through
   the base's centre, spin it, and look through the slits.

## History and sources

The British mathematician William George Horner published a slotted cylinder for moving pictures
in January 1834 and called it the Dædaleum. The American inventor William E. Lincoln made the
definitive version, with the slits above replaceable picture strips, and named it the zoetrope
("wheel of life"); his US patent was granted on 23 April 1867. A lamp with a turning band of birds
and animals is attributed to Ding Huan in ancient China, although it is unclear whether it gave the
illusion of motion. Source: Wikipedia, «Zoetrope», https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoetrope. The
drawings here are original.
