## What it is

A paper helicopter (also a whirlybird or rotocopter) is a strip of paper with two blades at the top
and a weight at the bottom. Dropped from a height, it starts to spin and falls much more slowly than
the same paper crumpled: the rising air turns the blades, which is autorotation, the state in which
a rotor "turns by the action of air moving up through the rotor" (Wikipedia, «Autorotation»). It is
a classic of science classrooms because every dimension changes the flight.

## Geometry

One strip, `width` wide:

- **Blades**: the top `blade_length` is cut down the middle, giving two blades of half the width.
- **Waist**: a band the full width, a quarter of the width tall, below the blades.
- **Body**: below the waist, the middle third of the width, `body_length` long; the corners on
  both sides are cut away.
- **Weight**: the bottom fifth of the body folds up (180°) to add weight low down.

The blade folds run across the strip where the cut ends. Each folds 90° − `pitch`, one towards you
(valley) and one away (mountain), so the blades stand out on opposite sides, tilted `pitch` above
the horizontal.

## The mechanism

- **Making it.** The fold program has three steps: the left blade towards you, the right blade
  away, the weight up. It is kinematic.
- **Spinning.** The strip is a kinematic sheet turned by a `hinge` mechanism about the body's
  vertical centre line on the `spin` control (0–360°), as an illustration of the spin.

**Preview and engine.** The folding and the illustrative spin play in the studio preview. The real
flight needs the engine's air model (wave 3): the lift and drag on the blades, the spin rate, the
slow descent, and the way blade length, pitch and weight change them.

## Making one by hand

1. Export the strip (`svg`, `pdf`, `dxf` or `hpgl`) and cut its outline and the centre cut.
2. Fold one blade towards you and the other away along the line where the cut ends.
3. Fold the bottom of the body up; a paper clip there adds weight and steadies the fall.
4. Hold it by the body, blades up, and let go from as high as you safely can.

## Parameters

|---|---|---|---|
| `blade_length` | length of the blades (the centre cut) | 80 mm | 24–360 mm (clamped to 0.8–3 × `width`) |

| `body_length` | length of the narrow body | 70 mm | 18–240 mm (clamped to 0.6–2 × `width`) |

Presets: `long-blades` (120 mm, 8°), `mini` (36 mm strip).

## History and sources

The paper helicopter is a traditional classroom toy with no single designer. It is also used to
teach experimental design and optimisation: students vary its dimensions and time its fall
(T. Siorek and R. Haftka, "Paper Helicopter - Experimental Optimum Engineering Design Classroom
Problem", 7th AIAA/USAF/NASA/ISSMO Symposium on Multidisciplinary Analysis and Optimization, 1998,
doi:10.2514/6.1998-4963). The flight principle is autorotation (Wikipedia, «Autorotation»,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autorotation). The geometry is computed by this cartridge.
