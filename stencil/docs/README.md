## What it is

A stencil is a plate with shapes cut through it: lay it on a surface, dab or spray paint through
the openings, lift it, and the shapes stay behind. Letters are the hard part. Cut an O out of a
plate and its middle falls out, and with it the inside of the letter. A stencil letter is
therefore cut as separate strokes with **bridges** of plate material between them, so every
island stays tied to the plate. This cartridge cuts any short text in a stroke alphabet it
generates itself.

## Geometry

**The glyphs** (A–Z, 0–9 and the space) are not taken from a font file. Each is a few stroke
centrelines, straight segments and elliptic arcs, in a unit box of cap height 1. Each is swept to a
stroke width of 0.16 with square ends and mitred joins; at a corner sharper than 120° the outside
is bevelled. Some strokes are marked as trimmable at an end, where they meet another stroke. A
fitting step trims those ends back, a few hundredths of a millimetre at a time, until every pair of
strokes in the glyph is at least the bridge apart. On a tie (the two halves of an O, the apex of
an A) both ends are trimmed, which keeps the letter symmetric. Closed bowls (B, D, O, P, Q, R,
0, 8) are cut as two halves with a bridge at the top and the bottom, or are attached to the stem by
a bridge. The script refuses any other character and names the glyph set.

**The plate** places the glyphs on a baseline with a fixed spacing, inside a margin of half the
letter height (at least 12 mm), with a 25 mm grip strip along the top behind a score line.
`pliego.papercut.analyze` must find one connected plate with every bridge at least the material's
minimum feature (1.0 mm for PET film; 1.5 mm for polypropylene and 160 g/m² card; 2.0 mm for
250 g/m² card). The bridge is never set below that minimum, and the cartridge refuses a bridge wider
than about an eighth of the letter height, where the strokes would be trimmed away.

## The fold program

A stencil plate is used flat, and nothing in it moves. The one fold is the scored grip strip: the
program folds it up, a handle to lift the plate off wet paint without dragging it, and then back
down so the plate lies flat for stencilling. The studio preview plays it.

## Making one by hand

1. Export the PDF or SVG at full size.
2. Cut PET film or polypropylene with a sharp craft knife on a mat, or let a plotter, craft
   cutter or laser cut it (PET and polypropylene give the most durable plates; card is fine for a
   few uses).
3. Score the grip line and fold the strip up to lift the plate.
4. Hold the plate flat and dab paint through it with a stiff brush or sponge, working from the
   edges of each opening inward.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `text` | up to 16 characters from A–Z, 0–9 and the space (lower case is set in capitals) |
| `height` | letter height, mm |
| `bridge` | width of the bridges between strokes, mm (never below the material's minimum feature) |
| `stock` | PET film, polypropylene, or cardstock 250 or 160 g/m² |

## History and sources

Stencilling is an old and widespread way of reproducing shapes, and stencil lettering with bridges
is a familiar industrial and craft style. This cartridge describes only the geometry it generates
and makes no historical claim. The glyphs are original and are not drawn from any published
stencil typeface.

- Wikipedia, "Stencil": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stencil
