## What it is

Two modes:

- **`cup`**: the folded paper cup. A square is folded in half along a diagonal, each side corner
  is folded across to the opposite edge and the two top flaps are tucked down, one in front and one
  behind. Pinch the sides and the mouth opens.
- **`cone`**: the drinking cone (*cucurucho*), the square rolled into a cone about the middle of one
  edge.

## Geometry

**The cup.** Let `d = s/√2` be half the diagonal. After the diagonal fold the triangle has corners
`(−d, 0)`, `(d, 0)` and `(0, d)`. The right corner is folded across so that it lands on the left edge
**and** its folded top edge comes out level. Reflecting the 45° right edge to the horizontal needs a
fold line at 67.5°. Landing the corner on the left edge fixes that line: it runs from
`((3 − 2√2) d, 0)` to `((√2 − 1) d, (2 − √2) d)`, and the corner lands at `(−(√2 − 1) d, (2 − √2) d)`.
The left corner is folded the same way, mirrored; its fold line passes exactly through the tip of
the first flap. The top flaps fold down at height `(2 − √2) d`. The flat cup is a trapezoid
`(2 − √2) d` high, `2(√2 − 1) d` wide at the mouth and `2(3 − 2√2) d` at the base.

**The cone.** The square is rolled about `A`, the middle of its bottom edge. A right circular cone of
half-angle α develops one turn into a sector of 360° · sin α. The half-disc of paper around `A`
(180°) therefore wraps 180 / (360 sin α) turns: 1.33 turns at the default 22°. The preview
approximates the curved surface with radial facet lines, 12 per turn of the cone. Each line is set to
the dihedral of a regular 12-sided pyramid of that half-angle, so the facets close exactly. Real
paper curves smoothly; the facet lines are written as flat (`F`) lines.

## The fold program

`cup` (kinematic, 5 steps): diagonal fold, right corner, left corner, front flap, back flap. Each
fold is an operation of `pliego.FoldProgram` on a layer-ordered flat state; after each one the
kernel checks the layer order (taco and tortilla conditions, transitivity) and Kawasaki and Maekawa
at every vertex, and the crease pattern is the final state unfolded. The front flap is a real tuck
(`FoldProgram.tuck`): it goes into the pocket in front, under the top layer there (the corner folded
last), instead of lying on top of it. With paper of no thickness it travels on the simple fold's
path and crosses that layer at the end; the kernel reports the crossing and finds no other paper in
the way. Every state is an exact rigid state (closure below 10⁻¹² mm). Opening the cup's mouth
curves the paper, which needs the physics engine; the program ends flat and the studio shows the cup
standing upright.

`cone` (kinematic, 1 step): the facet lines are scored flat first; then every facet turns at once,
each a hinge pose about its line carrying the paper beyond it, so the flat square rolls into the
cone. The facet lines are a polygonal model of a smooth roll, not creases.

**Fabrication note.** The kernel exports flat (`F`) lines on the score layer, so the cone's facet
lines appear there as rolling guides. Do not score them; there is not yet a way to author a hinge
line that is never drawn (an open question for the kernel).

## Making one by hand

Cup:

1. Fold a square in half along a diagonal, point up.
2. Fold the right corner across to touch the left edge so that its top edge is level.
3. Fold the left corner across to the right edge the same way.
4. Fold the front top flap down into the pocket in front; fold the back flap down behind.
5. Pinch the sides to open the cup.

Cone: roll the square into a cone around the middle of one edge, tuck the loose top corner inside
the rim, and hold it point down.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `size` | side of the square, mm |

## History and sources

The Public Paperfolding History Project finds the folded cup as "A Paper Wine Glass" in *Zhezhi
Xinfa* (New Ways to Fold Paper, The Commercial Press, China, 1934) and in Isao Honda's *Origami
Shuko* (1944). The rolled paper cone is an anonymous folk technique, credited here to tradition.

- The Public Paperfolding History Project, "The paper cup":
  https://www.origamiheaven.com/historyofthepapercup.htm
