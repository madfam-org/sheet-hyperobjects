## What it is

*Jianzhi* is Chinese paper-cut. Paper-cuts made to decorate windows are called window flowers
(*chuanghua*), and many paper-cut designs are symmetric because the paper is folded before it is
cut. This cartridge works in that idiom. A red square is folded through its centre into a narrow
wedge, cut once through every layer and unfolded into a round, radially symmetric design. The
motifs are original, generic geometric shapes generated by `main.py`. The cartridge reproduces no
traditional artwork and does not stand in for the work of paper-cut artists.

## Geometry

Every fold passes through the centre of the square (`pliego.simplefold` tracks each layer):

- **4-fold**: in half, in half again, then along the diagonal: a 45° wedge of 8 layers;
- **6-fold**: along the diagonal, a third over the middle and a third behind, then in half: a 30°
  wedge of 12 layers;
- **8-fold**: the 4-fold wedge folded in half once more: 22.5° and 16 layers.

A wedge of `180°/n` unfolds with `n`-fold rotation and `n` mirror lines (the dihedral group
D`n`). The script reads the wedge from the folded stack and refuses folds that do not make it.

The cut is drawn once, in polar coordinates inside the wedge, and `pliego.foldcut` maps it back
through every layer:

- a round outer edge with two scallops per wedge and a small hole in each scallop;
- a round hole at the centre of a solid hub;
- a ring of pointed petals, one on the axis of each wedge;
- **Blossom**: small diamonds on the mirror lines, then an outer ring of crescents on the wedge
  axes and round holes on the mirror lines;
- **Arches**: an outer ring of round-topped windows on the wedge axes, with diamonds on the
  mirror lines.

Sizes scale with the width of the wedge, so the 8-fold version keeps its bridges.
`pliego.papercut.analyze` must find one connected piece with every bridge at least the stock's
minimum feature (1.5 mm for tissue, 1.0 mm for kami, washi and copy paper); otherwise the cartridge
stops with a message.

## The fold program

The program re-folds the cut sheet along its creases in the original order, one step per fold,
then opens it and presses it flat: the hand unfolds the paper and holds it, as a window flower is
pasted flat. It does not let go. Following spec §5.6, a release would leave every crease standing
at about the paper's yield angle; that is the paper's memory, not how the finished piece is
shown. Version 1 documents describe the sheet after cutting, so the preview shows the folding on
the cut paper; in the real craft the cut is made while the paper is folded.

## Making one by hand

1. Fold a square of thin red paper as in the program: tissue or kami folds into many layers best.
2. Transfer one wedge of the exported cut lines onto the top layer.
3. Cut with small sharp scissors, or with a craft knife on a mat for the inner holes.
4. Unfold slowly and press flat under a book before pasting it on a window.

A plotter, craft cutter or laser can instead cut the flat, unfolded pattern from the SVG, DXF or
HPGL export.

## History and sources

Chinese paper-cut was inscribed in 2009 on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural
Heritage of Humanity [1]. UNESCO describes paper-cuts used for interior decoration, windows
included, and for festivities. It describes the craft as transmitted from mother to daughter, with
motifs and styles that vary by region [1]. The term window flowers (*chuanghua*), and the folding of
the paper before cutting to obtain symmetric designs, are described in [2]. The credit for the
tradition belongs to the communities who practise Chinese paper-cut. This cartridge makes no claim
beyond these sources.

1. UNESCO, "Chinese paper-cut", inscribed 2009 (decision 4.COM 13.09):
   https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chinese-paper-cut-00219
2. Wikipedia, "Chinese paper cutting": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_paper_cutting
