## What it is

A spinning top balances on its point while it turns. This one needs no stick or pin: the axle is
folded from a strip of card into a triangular tube with a pyramid point, and the disc has a
triangular hole that keys it onto the axle so that both turn together. The disc carries one of
three original patterns: seven colour sectors that blend towards white when spun (a Newton disc),
a two-arm spiral, or concentric rings.

## Geometry

- **Axle**: three panels `axle_width` (w) wide and `axle_length` long fold 120° each into a
  prism with an equilateral cross-section; a glue tab closes it. Under each panel an isosceles
  triangle makes one face of the point: with the in-radius r = w / (2√3) and the point a depth
  d = 1.4 w below the prism, each face is w wide and √(d² + r²) tall, and folds in by atan(r / d)
  (about 11.7°). The script checks that the folded axle is an isometry of the net and that its
  print faces outwards.
- **Disc**: `diameter`, with an equilateral hole the size of the axle's cross-section plus 0.2 mm,
  slid on 0.3 × the axle length above the point.

## The mechanism

- **Spinning** (mode `spin`): the assembled top (the axle's glue seam and the initial frame) turns
  on a `hinge` mechanism about the axle's centre line on the `spin` control.
- **Making it** (mode `make`): the fold program folds the prism, then the point.

**Preview and engine.** Both play in the studio preview. A real top's behaviour needs the physics
engine: the gyroscopic effect that keeps it upright, the first wobble that friction at the tip
settles, the precession that grows as it slows, and the final topple.

## Making one by hand

1. Export the sheets on card; the `svg` carries the disc's pattern.
2. Cut the axle, score its lines, fold the three panels into a tube and glue the tab inside. Fold
   the three tip faces in so they meet in a point; a drop of glue holds them.
3. Cut the disc and its triangular hole, and slide it onto the axle, about a third of the way up.
4. Twirl the axle between finger and thumb and let go on a smooth table.

| `axle_width` | width of each axle face | 8 mm | 5–15 mm |

Presets: `spiral`, `large-rings` (120 mm disc).

## History and sources

Spinning tops are among the oldest toys found by archaeologists and arose independently in many
cultures; a top balances on its tip by the gyroscopic effect, wobbling at first until friction at
the tip steadies it, and topples as friction slows it and its precession grows (Wikipedia,
«Spinning top», https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinning_top). The seven-colour disc follows the
Newton disc, which looks white or grey when spun fast, although Newton himself probably never used
a spinning disc (Wikipedia, «Newton disc», https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_disc). The design
here is computed by the cartridge.
