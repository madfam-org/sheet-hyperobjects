## What it is

A thaumatrope ("wonder turner") is a disc with a picture on each side and a string through each
side of a diameter. Twist the strings and pull: the disc spins about that diameter, both pictures
flash past many times a second, and the eye sees them together. The classic pair is a bird on one
side and a cage on the other. This cartridge draws three original pairs: `bird-cage`, `fish-bowl`
and `flower-pot`.

## Geometry

A disc of `diameter`, with two 3 mm string holes on its horizontal diameter, 8 % of the diameter
(at least 5 mm) in from the rim. The pictures are original path drawings made by the script and
printed as the disc's art. The front picture is drawn upright as seen from the front. The back one
is drawn upside down, because flipping the disc about a horizontal axis turns what is on the back
over: it then stands upright above or around the front picture.

## The mechanism

The disc is a kinematic sheet turned by a `hinge` mechanism about the string axis (the horizontal
diameter through the holes) on the `flip` control (0–360°). Sweeping the control shows each side in
turn.

**Preview and engine.** The flip plays in the studio preview. The merging of the two pictures is
not in the paper but in the viewer, at many turns a second; the twisted strings that drive a real
thaumatrope need the physics engine.

## Making one by hand

1. Export the disc. The `svg` carries the front picture; print the back picture (from the
   document's art) on the reverse, lining up the holes, or draw your own pair.
2. Cut the disc from card and punch the two holes.
3. Tie a loop of string through each hole. Hold the strings, wind the disc round a dozen times,
   then pull the strings tight and watch.

## History and sources

The thaumatrope is usually credited to the British physician John Ayrton Paris, who popularised it
in 1825 and described it in *Philosophy in Sport made Science in Earnest* (1827), with illustrations
by George Cruikshank. Charles Babbage recalled a competing story: the geologist William Henry Fitton
made a card disc on two silk threads after John Herschel showed a spinning-coin trick. A bird on
one side and a cage on the other is the classic pair. Source: Wikipedia, «Thaumatrope»,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaumatrope. The pictures here are original drawings.
